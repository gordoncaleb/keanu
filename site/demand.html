How much stock do we need next month?

Order too little and customers leave empty-handed; order too much and cash sits on the shelf. Keanu learns a product's trend, its weekly rhythm and how noisy its sales are from a year of history, then simulates thousands of possible months to find the stock that covers demand as often as you need.

How Keanu answered this

The history is a year of daily unit sales for one product. Keanu fits a negative binomial model on the log scale: a level of demand that follows a steady trend but also wanders from one four-week period to the next, an effect for each day of the week, and a dispersion that lets sales vary more than a Poisson count would. 

sales[day] ~ NegativeBinomial(exp(level[period] + weekday[day]), α)
level[p] = level[p − 1] + trend + σ z[p]      z ~ N(0, 1)
weekday effects sum to zero

level ~ N(log mean sales, 1)     yearly trend ~ N(0, 1)
σ ~ LogNormal(log 0.05, 0.6)     weekday ~ N(0, 0.5)
log α ~ N(2.5, 1)

The No-U-Turn Sampler draws 300 plausible sets of parameters in your browser. Within a four-week period each weekday has the same expected sales, so the model adds up those days and uses one term per period and weekday, which keeps the fit to a second or two. For every draw Keanu then plays the next eight weeks forward 20 times, letting the level keep wandering and drawing each day's sales, for 6,000 possible futures.

The stock you need is the quantile of total demand over your period at your service level, so it carries the uncertainty in the trend and the weekly pattern as well as day-to-day noise. Changing the period, the service level or the stock on hand reuses the same futures, so the answer updates without refitting.

The sales are synthetic, generated with known values: 40 units a day at the start, a trend of about 23% a year, steps of 5% between periods, weekends busier than weekdays, and a dispersion of 10. The weekday chart marks the true effects, and the demand chart marks what actually sold over your period in held-out weeks from the same process.

Keanu is a probabilistic programming library in Rust. Results are illustrations, not inventory advice.
