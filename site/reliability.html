How Keanu answered this

The history is 500 pumps installed over the past five years, about a third of them under high load. Some have failed, at a known age. The rest are still running, so all we know is that each will last longer than its age so far. Keanu fits a Weibull lifetime with a shape shared by the fleet and a scale that high load shortens: 

lifetime[i] ~ Weibull(k, η × exp(γ × high load[i]))
failed pump:  observed at its age when it failed
running pump: censored, lifetime > its age today
log k ~ N(ln 1.5, 0.5)    log η ~ N(ln 60, 1)    γ ~ N(0, 0.5)

A failed pump contributes the chance of failing at exactly its age; a running pump contributes the chance of surviving at least to its age, through Keanu's censored distribution. The No-U-Turn Sampler draws 500 plausible lifetime curves in your browser. For each one, a new pump fails within the warranty with a probability averaged over the load mix, so the claims on the new fleet are binomial. Keanu simulates them to give the likely range and the chance of going over budget. Changing the warranty, fleet, cost or budget reuses the fitted draws, so the answer updates without refitting.

Why the running pumps matter: drop them and keep only the failures, and the data hold only the lifetimes short enough to have ended already. Every pump seems to fail within five years, so the fit is far too pessimistic, and the warranty looks much more expensive than it is. The dashed grey curve and the hollow rings show that fit. Pumps still running are evidence of long lives, and censoring counts them as such.

The fleet is synthetic, generated with a known Weibull shape of 2 and a 72-month scale (43 months under high load), so you can see the model recover the true curve.

Keanu is a probabilistic programming library in Rust. Results are illustrations, not engineering advice.
