How much could this position lose tomorrow?

Volatility clusters: calm days follow calm days, and turbulent days follow turbulent ones. Keanu fits a volatility model to daily returns, right here in your browser, and turns the uncertainty in tomorrow's volatility, and in the model itself, into a loss you can plan for.

How Keanu answered this

Both models say a day's return is normal with mean zero and a volatility that changes from day to day. They differ in how it changes. 

GARCH(1,1)
  σ²ₜ = ω + α r²ₜ₋₁ + β σ²ₜ₋₁            today's variance from yesterday's shock and variance
  (α, β, 1 − α − β) ~ Dirichlet(2, 6, 2)    keeps the process stationary
  ω ~ HalfNormal(1)

Stochastic volatility
  hₜ = μ + φ (hₜ₋₁ − μ) + σ εₜ          the log variance is itself random
  μ ~ N(0, 10)   (φ + 1)/2 ~ Beta(20, 1.5)   σ ~ HalfCauchy(5)
  rₜ ~ N(0, exp(hₜ / 2))

Keanu samples each model's posterior with the No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) in WebAssembly, a few steps at a time in a background thread. GARCH has three parameters and fits in well under a second. Stochastic volatility has a latent volatility for every day, so it takes longer, and it captures volatility that moves on its own rather than only in response to returns.

For tomorrow, every posterior draw simulates the next day's volatility and then 20 returns. Value at risk is a quantile of those losses and expected shortfall is the mean loss beyond it, so both include the uncertainty in the parameters as well as in tomorrow's return.

Keanu is a probabilistic programming library in Rust. Results are illustrations, not investment advice.
