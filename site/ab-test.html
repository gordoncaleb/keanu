How Keanu answered this

Each version has an unknown conversion rate, and its conversions are what you would expect from that many visitors at that rate. Before the test, every rate from 0% to 100% is equally plausible. 

rate[A], rate[B] ~ Uniform(0, 1)
conversions[v] ~ Binomial(visitors[v], rate[v])

The No-U-Turn Sampler draws 1,000 plausible pairs of rates in your browser, in a fraction of a second, so the answer refits as you type. The chance B is better is the share of draws where B's rate is higher; the lift is B's rate over A's.

The expected loss of shipping a version is the conversion rate you give up, on average, in the draws where the other version is better. It weighs how likely a mistake is by how much it would cost, which a p-value alone does not. A common rule is to ship once the expected loss falls below the smallest difference you care about.

Keanu is a probabilistic programming library in Rust. Results are illustrations, not professional advice.
