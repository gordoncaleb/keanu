How many of our loans will default, and what will it cost?

A lender's repayment history says how often each risk grade defaults, but not exactly. Keanu learns those rates with their uncertainty, then prices the loans on the book today: how many could go bad, and how much money is at stake if the economy turns.

How Keanu answered this

The history is 3,000 past loans, each with a risk grade from A to E, a debt-to-income ratio, and whether it defaulted. Keanu fits a hierarchical logistic regression: each grade has its own default rate, but the grades share information, so a grade with few loans borrows strength from the others. 

logit P(default) = α[grade] + β × debt-to-income + stress
α[grade] = μ + τ z[grade]
z ~ N(0, 1)    μ ~ N(−2.5, 1.5)    τ ~ HalfNormal(1)    β ~ N(0, 1)

The No-U-Turn Sampler draws 500 plausible sets of rates in your browser. For each one, every open loan has its own default probability, so the number of defaults follows a Poisson-binomial distribution. Keanu computes its tail exactly to give the chance of more than your threshold, then simulates which loans default, at 45% of the amount outstanding lost on each, for the loss distribution.

The stress adds to every loan's log-odds of default, a simple stand-in for a downturn. Changing it reuses the fitted draws, so it updates without refitting. The history and the book are synthetic, generated with known default rates so you can see the model recover them.

Keanu is a probabilistic programming library in Rust. Results are illustrations, not credit advice.
